(02) Method

Discover, plan, deliver, hand over

  1. 01

    Discover

    We talk to the people who use the system and read what exists: contracts, tools, data and past incidents. No changes yet.

  2. 02

    Plan

    We write down the options, costs and risks in plain language and agree an order of work that fits your budget.

  3. 03

    Deliver

    Small increments, shipped and visible every week, so you can steer before money goes in the wrong place.

  4. 04

    Hand over

    Documentation, training and a short list of next steps. You keep everything; nothing depends on us staying.

2026 / Technology strategy

Technology roadmap

A founder-led company with a dozen tools and no plan, given a costed roadmap that leadership approved in one meeting.

Role
Advisory, roadmap
Timeline
4 weeks
Stack
Workshops, cost model, vendor review
0tools reviewed
0lower yearly software spend
0quarters of work, ordered

Sample case file. Replace with a real engagement.

The question

Every team had bought its own tools. Nobody could say what was used, what it cost or what depended on what, so each new request turned into an argument.

What we found and did

We mapped what ran, who used it and what it cost, then ranked the options by effect and effort. The roadmap retired four tools, kept the rest and set an order of work with owners. Leadership approved it in a single meeting.

The question

Dispatchers worked from a dense table that took seconds to load and hid the one number they needed. Training new staff took weeks.

2025 / Integration

Order-to-cash sync

A web store, an ERP and an accounting tool that disagreed every month-end, joined into one flow that reconciles itself.

Role
Integration, automation
Timeline
7 weeks
Stack
Node, Postgres, webhooks
0days faster month-end close
0orders synced first time
0re-keyed invoices

Sample case file. Replace with a real engagement.

The question

Orders were copied by hand from the store into the ERP, and invoices from the ERP into accounting. Month-end took a week of hunting for mismatches.

What we found and did

We drew the real flow, made one system the source of truth for each record and connected them with retried, logged jobs. A daily list shows anything that failed to sync, so exceptions are handled in minutes instead of found at month-end.

2026 / AI adoption

Support assistant

An assistant that drafts replies for a support queue, with a person approving every one before it is sent.

Role
AI workflow, evaluation
Timeline
6 weeks
Stack
LLM API, Python, Postgres
0less time per ticket
0drafts accepted after review

Sample case file. Replace with a real engagement.

The question

Support answered the same twenty questions all day. The team had tried a chatbot and switched it off after it invented a refund policy.

What we found and did

We started with drafts, not autonomy: the assistant proposes a reply with the sources it used, and an agent approves or edits it. A set of real past tickets scores every change, and the share of drafts accepted is the one number we track.
